# Maths teaching design review

This is an AI teaching-design review of the supplied book and the application's content and interactions. A human mathematics teacher was not consulted. Passing code, build and answer-key checks does not demonstrate that a child understands a concept; a classroom or parent observation remains a separate form of validation.

## What the first implementation missed

All 19 introductions originally used the same instruction, "Tap every discovery," and unlocked after three whole-card visits. This recorded taps rather than counting, changing a quantity, sorting or explaining a comparison. The same generic unit hint was repeated for different cards. Unit 1 showed pictures of apples but did not let the child mark each apple once, establish a final count or observe the same objects being rearranged. Numeric questions generated by `number()` always put the correct answer in the second position. These were real teaching-design defects despite correct source coverage and functioning navigation.

The revision follows the supplied book's purpose: play with a mathematical idea before answering written questions. The NCETM distinguishes reciting number words from understanding quantity and emphasises linking a completed count to the total. Its Year 1 teaching material supports counting objects once each and describing the last count as the group total. [NCETM: Cardinality and number sense](https://ncetm.org.uk/features/cardinality-and-number-sense/), [NCETM: Year 1 comparison of quantities and measures](https://www.ncetm.org.uk/media/c1kpuii5/ncetm_mm_sp1_y1_se01_teach.pdf).

EEF guidance supports moving and interacting with mathematical objects while explicitly connecting those actions to the concept and its pictures or symbols. Adding decorative images alone would not repair the missing mathematical action. [EEF: Use manipulatives and representations to develop understanding](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/early-years/maths/use-manipulatives-and-representations-to-develop-understanding).

## Unit 1 replacement

The first screen is **Count for Owl**. Its short goal is to count each object once, say how many altogether, and make a group of ten. Within the existing ungraded intro step:

1. **Count seven apples:** tap each apple once. A tap marks an individual apple and counts it once; tapping a marked apple does not add another count. After all are counted, choose the total. Feedback explicitly links the last count, seven, to seven apples altogether.
2. **Same seven, rearranged:** none are added or removed. The child chooses how many after the same quantity is spread out. Feedback connects unchanged quantity to the absence of additions/removals, rather than merely praising a correct button.
3. **Make ten:** add or remove counters to fill a ten frame with exactly ten counters. Confirm the constructed group. Feedback links the ten filled spaces, the group quantity and the number ten.

These are guided practice activities, not extra scored book questions. They prepare the original Unit 1 apple/star counts, missing numbers, dot recognition and written numeral questions. The book's source IDs and graded answers remain unchanged.

The Unit 6 introduction no longer substitutes a future analog-clock skill for time-of-day vocabulary. Unit 10 no longer uses seven identical unlabeled circles for weekdays or unrelated shapes for mass. Cube faces in Unit 15 are shown separately so the child is not told to count invisible faces in a single projection. These changes address specific content problems identified in the review.

## Contract and remaining practical checks

The existing lesson player, progress namespace, assessment engine and question IDs remain the same. Intro steps retain `kind: explore` and their stable step IDs. `mathWorkshop` holds `title`, `goal`, `scene` and three task records. Count tasks include a total, object type, arrangement, confirmation choices and `correctId`. Build tasks include `initialCount`, `target`, object type and a maximum count; the target is validation data and must not reveal an unknown answer in the prompt. Choose tasks include a clear question, optional neutral visual, choices, `correctId`, hint and feedback. The root Maths workshop widget completes and saves the activity; legacy picture-card touch counts are not evidence of workshop completion.

All source questions retain their original answer semantics. Runtime choice ordering is deterministic and preserves IDs, with numeric correct answers now distributed across positions. Content version 2 prevents treating an old three-card visit as completed new practice.

Implementation checks should verify that repeated object taps do not increase the count, counts use the number of marked objects rather than object index, rearrangement does not add/remove objects, initial build groups survive resume, ten-frame spaces have one counter each, and the next step stays unavailable until all three tasks are complete. These checks establish correct software behavior; observing a six-year-old follow the instructions and explain the quantity would provide evidence of actual teaching usability. This AI review does not claim that such a child trial or human-teacher consultation has occurred.
